\begin{abstract}
Task-relevant structure denotes the constraints that specify which visual elements a model must represent, how those elements should relate to one another, and which properties its output must preserve to satisfy the task. In visual understanding, it comprises pixel-grounded entities and their relations; in reference-conditioned generation, it captures pose, spatial correspondence, and identity consistency over time. Standard learning objectives may not enforce this structure, allowing models to exploit shortcuts that reduce training loss without satisfying the intended constraints. This thesis argues that reliable constraint satisfaction requires making task-relevant structure explicit and aligning learning with it through the representation, objective, or decoding procedure.

For visual understanding, this thesis adopts panoptic scene graph generation (PSG), which segments an image into panoptic entities and predicts subject--object--relation triplets over both ``things'' and ``stuff'' categories. Chapters~3--5 address three limitations of this structured representation. \emph{HiLo} introduces frequency-specialized branches and cross-branch consistency to improve long-tail relation prediction under semantic overlap. \emph{VLPrompt} augments object-pair features with context-rich language cues elicited from large language models and learns how to combine these priors with visual evidence. \emph{OpenPSG} moves PSG toward open-set relation prediction by combining open-set panoptic segmentation, learned pair filtering, and autoregressive multimodal relation decoding. Together, these methods align representation and prediction with the relational structure required by PSG under imbalanced supervision, ambiguous semantics, and an expanding label space.

For visual generation, this thesis studies reference-conditioned image and video synthesis, where the output must preserve the appearance and identity specified by one or more reference images. Chapters~6--7 make the relevant spatial and temporal structure actionable during learning. \emph{Leffa} interprets cross-attention as a soft correspondence field and regularizes it during training, directing diffusion models toward the appropriate reference regions and reducing fine-grained distortion in virtual try-on and pose transfer. \emph{Saber} constructs synthetic references from partially masked video frames and uses tailored attention to learn reference-to-video generation from ordinary video--text pairs. This removes the need to construct dedicated reference image--video--text triplets while promoting identity consistency across frames.

Across the five technical chapters, task-relevant structure takes different forms but serves the same function: it specifies the relationships and invariances that the model should preserve. The proposed methods operationalize this structure through complementary representations, objectives, and decoding procedures, thereby reducing shortcut behavior in the evaluated settings. The thesis consequently provides a unified account of structured scene understanding and reference-conditioned generation without requiring the two areas to share a single architecture or supervisory signal.
\end{abstract}
