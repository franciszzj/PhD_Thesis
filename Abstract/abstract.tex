\begin{abstract}
Visual intelligence manifests in two complementary capabilities: \emph{visual understanding}, which converts pixels into structured and queryable knowledge, and \emph{visual generation}, which synthesizes content that is both realistic and faithful to user intent. Although recent progress has been propelled by large-scale pretraining and diffusion models, two persistent bottlenecks remain. First, understanding systems often lack \emph{compositional structure}: they may detect objects yet fail to produce consistent relational descriptions that support reasoning, especially under long-tailed distributions and open-world vocabulary shifts. Second, generative systems often lack \emph{reliable controllability}: they can produce high-fidelity imagery, but may only partially follow instructions, entangle multiple constraints, or drift temporally in video.

This thesis, \emph{Visual Intelligence: Structured Understanding and Controllable Generation}, explores one representative direction for each capability. For understanding, we study \emph{panoptic scene graph generation} (PSG), a holistic task that unifies panoptic segmentation (pixel-accurate ``things'' and ``stuff'') with relation prediction to yield a structured scene graph grounded at the pixel level. Compared with box-based scene graphs, PSG anchors entities to masks and includes ``stuff'' regions, making it better suited for reasoning about contact, containment, and contextual relations that depend on precise geometry. PSG also serves as a rigorous testbed for structured recognition: models must bridge fine localization and high-level semantics, cope with rare but semantically critical predicates under severe long-tailed distributions, and ultimately generalize beyond closed-set label spaces. For generation, we study \emph{controllable image and video generation}, where synthesis must obey explicit constraints over content, layout, attributes, identity, and motion while preserving realism and temporal consistency.

Across five papers, Chapters~3--5 advance PSG from complementary perspectives. \emph{HiLo} targets a core but under-emphasized challenge in PSG: relations are long-tailed \emph{and} semantically overlapping, such that an entity pair may simultaneously admit coarse and fine predicates (e.g., \emph{beside} vs.\ \emph{chasing}). We introduce a principled architecture that specializes different branches for high- and low-frequency predicates, then fuses them via consistency constraints, enabling the model to recover informative rare relations without collapsing into frequency-driven shortcuts. HiLo is built on a strong one-stage PSG baseline that strengthens the interaction between mask prediction and relation decoding, and it improves both recall and mean recall on PSG while transferring gains to box-based scene graph generation.

\emph{VLPrompt} takes a different stance: instead of relying solely on visual cues, it treats language as a practical source of commonsense knowledge for structured prediction. We design a vision--language prompting pipeline that elicits context-rich descriptions from large language models for candidate relations and uses them as complementary evidence during training and inference. A dual-attention prompter integrates object-pair visual features with two types of language signals (proposed descriptions and judgement-oriented prompts), and a gating mechanism adaptively combines their predictions. This lightweight interface improves rare predicate recognition and generalization without requiring task-specific retraining when the label space evolves.

\emph{OpenPSG} pushes PSG further toward the open world. While recent multimodal pretraining enables open-set object detection and segmentation, open-set \emph{relation} prediction remains substantially harder due to combinatorial object pairing and the need for grounded interaction understanding. We propose the first open-set PSG formulation that combines an open-set panoptic segmenter with an efficient relation query transformer that extracts pairwise interaction features and estimates relation existence to prune irrelevant pairs. On top of these grounded visual tokens, a large multimodal model is prompted to generate relation phrases autoregressively under carefully designed generation and verification instructions, enabling scalable predicate prediction beyond predefined vocabularies.

Chapters~6--7 develop controllable generation methods for images and videos. \emph{Leffa} focuses on controllable person image generation (e.g., virtual try-on and pose transfer), where models must preserve fine-grained reference details (textures, logos, text) while respecting target pose and context. We identify a root cause of detail distortion in diffusion-based pipelines: attention often fails to align target queries with the correct reference regions. Leffa introduces a training-time regularization that interprets attention as a learnable flow field and explicitly guides cross-reference attention toward correct correspondences, improving faithfulness without adding parameters or inference cost and generalizing across diffusion backbones.

\emph{Saber} addresses scalable reference-to-video generation, where the goal is to synthesize temporally coherent videos that follow a text prompt while preserving subject identity from one or multiple reference images. Existing methods depend on expensive triplet datasets (reference images, videos, text), which limits scale and diversity. Saber bypasses this bottleneck with a zero-shot training paradigm: using only video--text pairs, it simulates reference conditioning through a masked training strategy that samples partially masked frames as references, combined with attention masks and spatial mask augmentations to reduce copy--paste artifacts. The resulting model generalizes across different numbers of references and achieves strong identity consistency and temporal coherence on public benchmarks.

Collectively, the thesis argues for a unified perspective: explicit structure is central to robust understanding (especially under long-tail and open-world shifts), and dependable controllability is essential for generation to become a practical, intent-driven visual interface.
\end{abstract}
