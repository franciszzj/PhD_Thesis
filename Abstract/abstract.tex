\begin{abstract}
Visual intelligence manifests in two complementary capabilities: \emph{visual understanding}, which converts pixels into structured and queryable knowledge, and \emph{visual generation}, which synthesizes content that is both realistic and faithful to user intent. Although recent progress has been propelled by large-scale pretraining and diffusion models, two persistent bottlenecks remain. First, understanding systems often lack \emph{compositional structure}: they may detect objects yet fail to produce consistent relational descriptions that support reasoning, especially under long-tailed distributions and open-world vocabulary shifts. Second, generative systems often lack \emph{reliable controllability}: they can produce high-fidelity imagery, but may only partially follow instructions, entangle multiple constraints, or drift temporally in video.

This thesis, \emph{Visual Intelligence: Structured Understanding and Controllable Generation}, explores one representative direction for each capability. For understanding, we study \emph{panoptic scene graph generation} (PSG), a holistic task that unifies panoptic segmentation (pixel-accurate ``things'' and ``stuff'') with relation prediction to yield a structured scene graph grounded at the pixel level. PSG serves as a rigorous testbed for structured recognition, requiring models to bridge fine localization and high-level semantics, to cope with rare but semantically critical predicates, and to generalize beyond closed-set label spaces. For generation, we study \emph{controllable image and video generation}, where synthesis must obey explicit constraints over content, layout, attributes, identity, and motion while preserving realism and temporal consistency.

Across five papers, Chapters~3--5 advance PSG from complementary perspectives. \emph{HiLo} improves structure modeling across semantic granularities to better reconcile fine visual evidence and coarse relational semantics. \emph{VLPrompt} introduces vision--language prompting as a lightweight, transferable mechanism that injects linguistic and commonsense priors into structured prediction. \emph{OpenPSG} pushes PSG toward open-vocabulary and open-set conditions by leveraging large multimodal models for scalable relational reasoning. Chapters~6--7 develop controllable generation methods for images and videos. \emph{Leffa} investigates principled control interfaces and intermediate representations that improve faithfulness without sacrificing perceptual quality. \emph{Saber} strengthens controllability under complex, multi-condition constraints with improved robustness and temporal coherence. Collectively, the thesis argues for a unified perspective: explicit structure is central to robust understanding, and dependable controllability is essential for generation to become a practical, intent-driven visual interface.
\end{abstract}
