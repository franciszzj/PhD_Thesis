\begin{abstract}
Visual intelligence demands two complementary capabilities: structured visual understanding that parses images into discrete entities and relations, and controllable visual generation that synthesizes content faithful to explicit user intent. Despite rapid progress, modern systems still struggle with \emph{structure}---capturing compositional semantics under open-world variation---and with \emph{control}---producing high-fidelity outputs while obeying multi-granular constraints.

This thesis, \emph{Visual Intelligence: Structured Understanding and Controllable Generation}, addresses these challenges through five papers spanning recognition and synthesis. Chapters~3--5 focus on structured visual understanding via \emph{panoptic scene graph generation} (PSG): \emph{HiLo} improves structure modeling across different semantic granularities, \emph{VLPrompt} studies vision--language prompting as a lightweight and transferable mechanism for structured prediction, and \emph{OpenPSG} advances PSG toward open-vocabulary and open-set conditions. Chapters~6--7 study controllable visual generation: \emph{Leffa} investigates control interfaces and representations for faithful controllable image and video synthesis, and \emph{Saber} strengthens controllability under complex constraints with improved robustness and generalization. Collectively, the thesis argues that explicit structure is central to reliable understanding, and that dependable controllability is essential for generation to serve as a practical visual interface.
\end{abstract}
