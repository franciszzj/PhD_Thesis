\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}

\section{Summary}
This thesis investigated two complementary facets of visual intelligence: structured visual understanding and controllable visual generation. For understanding, we studied panoptic scene graph generation (PSG)~\citep{yang2022panoptic}, which unifies panoptic segmentation~\citep{kirillov2019panoptic} and relational reasoning in the spirit of scene graph generation~\citep{lu2016visual} to produce a holistic, pixel-grounded scene representation. We developed three contributions that progressively extend PSG capability. \textbf{HiLo}~\citep{zhou2023hilo} revisits PSG under long-tailed relations and semantic overlap, improving structure modeling across semantic granularity to strengthen relation prediction while preserving localization fidelity. \textbf{VLPrompt}~\citep{zhou2023vlprompt} introduces vision--language prompting as a lightweight and transferable mechanism for structured prediction, leveraging linguistic priors from large language models~\citep{chatgpt, touvron2023llama} to complement visual evidence and improve generalization. \textbf{OpenPSG}~\citep{zhou2025openpsg} moves beyond closed-set assumptions and advances PSG toward open-vocabulary and open-set conditions, leveraging large multimodal models~\citep{radford2021learning, li2023blip} to scale relational reasoning to novel concepts.

For generation, we studied controllable image and video synthesis with diffusion models~\citep{ho2020denoising, rombach2022high}, where the objective is not merely realism but faithful satisfaction of explicit constraints over content, layout, attributes, identity, and motion. \textbf{Leffa}~\citep{zhou2025learning} improves reference-guided controllability by explicitly regularizing attention-based correspondence, reducing fine-grained detail distortion without additional inference cost. \textbf{Saber}~\citep{zhou2025scaling} strengthens identity-preserving reference-to-video generation in a scalable, zero-shot setting and emphasizes robustness and temporal coherence under multi-reference inputs. Together, these contributions support a consistent thesis: explicit structure is a reliable route to stronger understanding, and dependable controllability is a necessary condition for generation to become a practical visual interface.

\section{Lessons Learned}
Several themes recur across the chapters.
\textbf{(i) Structure should be explicit and multi-granular.} PSG benefits from representations that simultaneously capture fine geometry (masks, overlaps, boundaries) and coarse semantics (categories, contextual cues)~\citep{kirillov2019panoptic, yang2022panoptic}. Treating relations as an afterthought on top of detection is often insufficient; relations require their own inductive biases and training objectives~\citep{zellers2018neural}.
\textbf{(ii) Long-tail is not only a data issue but also a modeling issue.} Predicate imbalance interacts with semantic overlap and contextual bias. Methods that explicitly model complementary signals and enforce consistency can improve rare relations without collapsing to frequency-driven shortcuts~\citep{tang2020unbiased, zhou2023hilo}.
\textbf{(iii) Language is a pragmatic source of knowledge.} Vision--language pretraining and prompting provide a flexible interface for injecting priors, adapting to evolving label spaces, and improving reasoning when visual evidence is ambiguous~\citep{radford2021learning, min2023recent, zhou2023vlprompt}.
\textbf{(iv) Control must be treated as a first-class objective.} In generation, realism alone is not sufficient. A practical model must preserve invariants, respect edit locality, and resolve multiple constraints consistently. For diffusion models, this often requires explicit control interfaces and attention-based mechanisms~\citep{zhang2023adding, chefer2023attend}. For video, temporal coherence must be enforced as aggressively as spatial fidelity~\citep{blattmann2023stable, zhou2025scaling}.

\section{Limitations}
Despite progress, several limitations remain.
\textbf{PSG limitations:} (i) Rare relations and subtle interactions (e.g., fine contact, small objects, implicit functional relations) remain difficult when visual evidence is weak or occluded. (ii) Open-vocabulary settings introduce ambiguity in naming and grounding predicates; different paraphrases may be equally plausible, and evaluation becomes sensitive to language choices. (iii) PSG inherits errors from panoptic segmentation, and joint optimization remains challenging when segmentation and relation objectives compete.
\textbf{Generation limitations:} (i) Multi-condition control signals can conflict, leading to partial constraint satisfaction or unintended attribute leakage. (ii) Faithful editing can still over-modify backgrounds or distort identity under large appearance changes. (iii) For video, long-range temporal consistency remains fragile; drift can accumulate over long clips, and robustness under camera motion or occlusion is still limited.
\textbf{System limitations:} Many methods rely on heavy backbones or iterative inference, which constrains latency and energy efficiency, especially for video and high-resolution generation.

\section{Future Directions}
\subsection{Unified Structure-Aware Generation}
Bridging understanding and generation suggests models that use structured scene representations (panoptic entities, relations, and layouts) as native generative controls. A promising direction is \emph{structure-to-visual} synthesis where PSG-style graphs guide generation at multiple resolutions, enabling compositional scene construction and structure-preserving edits. Existing diffusion controls already show that adding structural conditions (e.g., edges, masks, depth) can substantially improve faithfulness~\citep{zhang2023adding, xie2023boxdiff}; extending these interfaces to relational graphs is a natural next step. Key research questions include how to represent relations in a differentiable manner that is compatible with diffusion denoising, how to expose editable subgraphs for localized edits, and how to maintain identity and style while changing relational structure.

\subsection{Open-World Structured Understanding}
Scaling PSG requires vocabulary-agnostic recognition of both entities and predicates. Promising paths include language-grounded predicate spaces with calibrated confidence, continual learning that adds new concepts without catastrophic forgetting, and self-training pipelines that mine pseudo-labels from large multimodal corpora. These directions connect closely to open-vocabulary recognition and prompting-based adaptation~\citep{wu2024towards, zhang2024vision, zhou2025openpsg}. Another important direction is evaluation: open-world PSG requires metrics and protocols that accommodate paraphrases, partial matches, and abstention, while still rewarding grounded, evidence-based predictions.

\subsection{Reliable Controllability for Video}
Future systems should combine multi-signal conditioning (text, layout, reference frames, motion cues) with long-range temporal reasoning. Recent video diffusion work has demonstrated both the promise of scaling and the persistence of temporal artifacts~\citep{blattmann2023stable, guo2023animatediff, peebles2023scalable}. Stronger temporal objectives that directly optimize constraint satisfaction, identity preservation, and stability over long horizons are likely to be critical, especially for reference-based control~\citep{liu2025phantom, yuan2025opens2v, zhou2025scaling}. From a system perspective, efficient adapters and caching strategies can reduce the cost of video diffusion while enabling user-specific controls and iterative editing.

\subsection{Evaluation and Benchmarks}
Beyond perception metrics, controllability should be measured by constraint satisfaction, identity preservation, edit locality, and temporal stability, with standardized test suites that stress compositional prompts and conflicting constraints. For PSG, benchmarks should emphasize rare relations, cross-dataset generalization, and open-world evaluation with language variability. Human-in-the-loop protocols are also important: they better capture instruction fidelity, usefulness, and failure modes that are difficult to quantify automatically.

\subsection{Efficiency and Adaptation}
Parameter-efficient tuning, modular control branches, and caching strategies are key to practical deployment. For PSG, lightweight adapters that expand vocabulary without retraining the full model can reduce maintenance cost as label spaces evolve. For generation, modular controls that can be composed (rather than retrained jointly) may improve reliability and reduce interference among constraints.

\subsection{Trustworthy Generation}
As control becomes more precise and accessible, safeguards become more important. Provenance tracking and watermarking can support attribution; edit auditing can help users understand what changed; and policy-aware constraints can reduce misuse. From a research standpoint, a key challenge is to integrate safety mechanisms without degrading controllability or encouraging brittle failure modes under benign edits.

\section{Closing Remarks}
The results in this thesis support a coherent direction for visual intelligence. Structured understanding is most useful when it is holistic, grounded, and scalable to open-world vocabularies; controllable generation is most valuable when it is faithful, compositional, and temporally stable. Advancing both---and connecting them through structure-aware control---is a promising route toward dependable systems that can parse complex scenes and render user intent in the open world.
