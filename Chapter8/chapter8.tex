\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}

\section{Summary}
This thesis addressed two unresolved problems in visual intelligence: richer pixel-grounded structured understanding and more reliable control mechanisms for image and video generation. For structured understanding, the thesis focused on panoptic scene graph generation (PSG)~\citep{yang2022panoptic}, which segments an image into panoptic entities and predicts subject--object--relation triplets to form a scene graph grounded in both ``things'' and ``stuff'' regions. Across Chapters~3--5, the thesis developed three complementary advances. \textbf{HiLo}~\citep{zhou2023hilo} improves long-tail relation recognition under semantic overlap through frequency-specialized branches and cross-branch consistency. \textbf{VLPrompt}~\citep{zhou2025vlprompt} augments grounded relation prediction with context-rich language cues elicited from large language models. \textbf{OpenPSG}~\citep{zhou2025openpsg} extends PSG beyond closed vocabularies by combining open-set panoptic segmentation with efficient pair filtering and autoregressive multimodal relation decoding.

For controllable generation, the thesis studied reference-conditioned image and video synthesis with diffusion models~\citep{ho2020denoising, rombach2022high}, where the challenge is to preserve user-specified appearance, identity, and motion constraints while maintaining realism. \textbf{Leffa}~\citep{zhou2025learning} introduces an attention regularization loss, instantiated through learnable flow fields in attention, to guide reference-conditioned diffusion models toward the correct source regions and reduce fine-grained detail distortion. \textbf{Saber}~\citep{zhou2025scaling} addresses reference-to-video generation with a scalable zero-shot strategy that learns from ordinary video--text pairs using masked training, attention design, and mask augmentation, preserving identity without requiring expensive reference image--video--text triplets.

Taken together, these contributions show that progress in the two thesis directions depends on making the right structure explicit: pixel-grounded entities and relations for understanding, and explicit preservation mechanisms for controllable generation. Overall, the thesis advances visual intelligence by improving long-tail robustness, language-assisted relation reasoning, and open-world scene understanding on one side, and by improving faithful reference preservation for image and video generation on the other.

\noindent \textbf{Contributions at a Glance.}
Across Chapters~3--7, the thesis contributions can be summarized as five algorithmic steps toward more dependable systems:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{HiLo: multi-granular long-tail PSG.} HiLo addresses a practical mismatch between annotation and semantics: relation labels are both imbalanced and overlapping. By specializing modeling capacity for head and tail predicates and enforcing consistency across granularities, HiLo improves tail relation prediction without sacrificing overall structure quality~\citep{zhou2023hilo}.
    \item \textbf{VLPrompt: language priors for relation prediction.} VLPrompt uses large language models to provide context-rich relation descriptions that complement visual evidence. Instead of replacing vision with language, it treats language as an auxiliary signal and learns when to trust it, improving generalization especially for rare predicates and ambiguous contexts~\citep{zhou2025vlprompt}.
    \item \textbf{OpenPSG: open-world grounded relation prediction.} OpenPSG extends PSG beyond a fixed predicate vocabulary by generating grounded relation phrases with multimodal models and efficiency-aware pair reasoning. This shifts the interface from closed-set classification to open-world grounded prediction~\citep{zhou2025openpsg}.
    \item \textbf{Leffa: attention regularization for reference preservation.} Leffa interprets cross-reference attention as a soft correspondence field and regularizes it during diffusion training through learnable flow fields. This improves detail preservation (textures, logos, fine patterns) under large pose or appearance changes without adding inference-time modules~\citep{zhou2025learning}.
    \item \textbf{Saber: scalable reference-to-video via simulated references.} Saber removes the reliance on expensive reference image--video--text triplets by training on ordinary video--text pairs and simulating reference conditioning through masked training, attention design, and augmentation. The resulting model better preserves identity over time and generalizes across different numbers of reference images~\citep{zhou2025scaling}.
\end{itemize}
\noindent The first three contributions address structured understanding, and the last two address controllable generation. Together they show that dependable visual systems require both richer grounded representations for reasoning and explicit preservation mechanisms for creation.

\section{Lessons Learned}
Four lessons recur across the chapters: PSG makes structured understanding concrete, long-tail robustness requires modeling choices, language helps only when grounded, and faithful controllability must be taught explicitly. The following paragraphs expand these lessons.

\noindent \textbf{(i) PSG Is Valuable Because It Makes Structure Explicit and Multi-Granular.}
In practice, ``explicit structure'' means that the model exposes intermediate elements that can be inspected, analyzed, and improved. For PSG, masks make grounding concrete, and relations make compositional semantics explicit. Multi-granularity matters because supervision and semantics rarely align perfectly: datasets may annotate a specific predicate, but several predicates can be valid at different levels of abstraction. Treating this as a first-class issue---rather than as label noise to be ignored---leads to designs that are more robust under semantic ambiguity and that can better support downstream reasoning~\citep{cheng2022visual, zhou2023hilo}.

\noindent \textbf{(ii) Long-Tail Robustness Is Also a Modeling and Optimization Issue.}
Long-tail performance depends not only on data frequency but on how models allocate capacity and how decision boundaries are calibrated. For relations, shortcuts are especially tempting: object labels and co-occurrence patterns can predict many head predicates without looking at the image. Methods like HiLo highlight the value of explicitly separating complementary signals (head vs.\ tail behavior) and enforcing consistency, so that rare predicates are not drowned out by frequent gradients~\citep{tang2020unbiased, zellers2018neural, zhou2023hilo}. This perspective also suggests that evaluation protocols (\eg, mean recall) should be treated as part of the problem definition rather than as an afterthought~\citep{zellers2018neural}.

\noindent \textbf{(iii) Language Is Useful When It Is Grounded.}
Language models and vision--language pretraining offer a scalable source of priors, but naive usage can introduce hallucinations or overly generic relations~\citep{radford2021learning, min2023recent, zhang2024vision}. The thesis experience suggests that language is most effective when used as a \emph{complement} to vision: as candidate descriptions, paraphrases, or priors that are fused with visual evidence, with mechanisms that decide when to trust language~\citep{zhou2025vlprompt}. This is particularly important in open-world settings, where the flexibility of language must be balanced against the need for evidence-based predictions and calibrated confidence~\citep{wu2024towards, zhang2024vision}.

\noindent \textbf{(iv) Faithful Controllability Requires Correspondence, Preservation Signals, and Scalable Supervision.}
Adding more conditions (text, masks, depth, references) does not automatically yield better control~\citep{zhang2023adding, xie2023boxdiff}. The key is whether the model can bind each condition to the right region and maintain that binding over the denoising trajectory and over time. Leffa and Saber emphasize this point from different angles: Leffa improves spatial correspondence through attention regularization, while Saber improves temporal identity consistency through masked training that forces the model to use reference information in a disciplined way even without explicit triplet supervision~\citep{zhou2025learning, zhou2025scaling}. This suggests that future progress in controllable generation will likely come from better preservation objectives, stronger temporal alignment, and training schemes that scale beyond highly curated datasets.

\section{Limitations}
Despite progress, several limitations remain.
\textbf{PSG limitations:} (i) Rare relations and subtle interactions (\eg, fine contact, small objects, implicit functional relations) remain difficult when visual evidence is weak or occluded. (ii) Open-world settings introduce ambiguity in naming and grounding predicates; different paraphrases may be equally plausible, and evaluation becomes sensitive to language choices. (iii) PSG inherits errors from panoptic segmentation, and joint optimization remains challenging when segmentation and relation objectives compete~\citep{yang2022panoptic, cheng2022visual, wu2024towards}.
\textbf{Generation limitations:} (i) Multi-condition control signals can conflict, leading to partial constraint satisfaction or unintended attribute leakage. (ii) Faithful reference preservation can still fail under large deformations, drastic viewpoint changes, or contradictory conditions. (iii) For video, long-range temporal consistency remains fragile; drift can accumulate over long clips, and robustness under camera motion or occlusion is still limited~\citep{zhang2023adding, zhou2025learning, zhou2025scaling, liu2025phantom}.
\textbf{System limitations:} The computational cost is concrete rather than abstract. In Chapter~6, the virtual try-on model is trained progressively at $512 \times 384$ for $10\text{k}$ steps, at $1024 \times 768$ for $36\text{k}$ steps, and then finetuned for another $10\text{k}$ steps with the \leffa{} loss; the latent diffusion backbone also uses an $8\times$ spatial compression, which makes extremely small details such as tiny text difficult to preserve~\citep{rombach2022high, zhou2025learning}. In Chapter~7, Saber is finetuned from the Wan2.1-14B video model, uses $50$ denoising steps at inference, and depends on a foreground segmenter for reference preprocessing, so reference-to-video generation remains expensive even when explicit reference image--video--text triplets are avoided~\citep{wan2025wan, zhou2025scaling}.

\noindent \textbf{Additional PSG Limitations.}
PSG also inherits limitations from the annotation process: relation labels can be incomplete, inconsistent across annotators, or biased toward visually salient interactions~\citep{cheng2022visual, zhu2022scene}. This makes it difficult to disentangle whether a model failed to reason or whether the dataset omitted a plausible relation. Moreover, PSG predictions are inherently combinatorial; even with pair pruning, scaling to dense scenes with many entities remains challenging~\citep{wang2023pair, zhou2025openpsg}. Finally, open-set PSG introduces new evaluation questions~\citep{wu2024towards, zhou2025openpsg}: how should we score semantically correct paraphrases, and how should abstention be rewarded when evidence is insufficient?

\noindent \textbf{Additional Generation Limitations.}
For controllable diffusion, correspondence regularization improves detail transfer, but it does not fully resolve cases where the reference and target differ dramatically (\eg, extreme occlusions, drastic viewpoint changes) or where the conditioning signals are contradictory~\citep{zhou2025learning}. Similarly, simulated reference training improves scalability, but it can still struggle with long-horizon coherence when videos contain rapid scene cuts, complex camera motion, or multiple interacting subjects~\citep{zhou2025scaling, liu2025phantom}. These failure modes point to the need for stronger temporal objectives and better mechanisms to represent persistent identity and scene structure over long times.

\section{Future Directions}
\subsection{Faithful Reference-Preserving Image Generation}
A direct next step for controllable image generation is to improve how models preserve fine-grained reference information under larger geometric and semantic changes. Existing diffusion controls already show that adding structural conditions (\eg, edges, masks, depth, pose) can improve faithfulness~\citep{zhang2023adding, xie2023boxdiff}, but preserving the \emph{right} appearance details under large viewpoint changes, occlusion, or multiple references remains difficult. Promising directions include better correspondence objectives, stronger disentanglement of editable versus invariant factors, and training data that expose more challenging transformations without sacrificing realism.

Another promising direction is to combine reference preservation with richer editable structure. Instead of treating reference images and control signals separately, future systems may jointly reason over who or what should stay fixed, which region should change, and how multiple constraints should be prioritized. This would move controllable generation closer to the explicit ``what to preserve, where to apply it'' view that emerged throughout Chapters~6--7.

\subsection{Open-World Structured Understanding}
Scaling PSG requires vocabulary-agnostic recognition of both entities and predicates. Promising paths include language-grounded predicate spaces with calibrated confidence, continual learning that adds new concepts without catastrophic forgetting, and self-training pipelines that mine pseudo-labels from large multimodal corpora. These directions connect closely to open-vocabulary recognition and prompting-based adaptation~\citep{wu2024towards, zhang2024vision, zhou2025openpsg}. Another important direction is evaluation: open-world PSG requires metrics and protocols that accommodate paraphrases, partial matches, and abstention, while still rewarding grounded, evidence-based predictions.

Beyond adding more vocabulary, open-world PSG must address \emph{distribution shift}: relations change with domains (indoor vs.\ outdoor, consumer photos vs.\ robotics), and long-tail predicates can be domain-specific~\citep{wu2024towards, zhang2024vision}. A practical direction is to combine retrieval and prompting, where a system retrieves relevant textual or visual exemplars for rare relations and uses them as context for prediction. Another direction is to improve calibration and abstention: models should be able to say ``unknown'' when evidence is weak, and should provide uncertainty estimates that can trigger human-in-the-loop correction.

\subsection{Reliable Controllability for Video}
Future systems should combine multi-signal conditioning (text, layout, reference frames, motion cues) with long-range temporal reasoning. Recent video diffusion work has demonstrated both the promise of scaling and the persistence of temporal artifacts~\citep{blattmann2023stable, guo2023animatediff, peebles2023scalable}. Stronger temporal objectives that directly optimize constraint satisfaction, identity preservation, and stability over long horizons are likely to be critical, especially for reference-based control~\citep{liu2025phantom, yuan2025opens2v, zhou2025scaling}. An equally important direction is to reduce reliance on specialized reference image--video--text triplets by designing scalable pretext tasks, masking schemes, and data constructions that teach reference awareness from weaker supervision.

In addition to better temporal modules, video controllability likely needs better \emph{memory}. Humans maintain stable mental models of characters and environments across shots; models should similarly maintain persistent identity tokens, appearance descriptors, and scene layout representations that can be reused across frames. This suggests hybrid architectures that combine diffusion with explicit memory banks or state-space models, as well as training objectives that penalize identity drift over long horizons. Another practical direction is interactive editing: instead of generating an entire video at once, systems can support iterative refinement where users correct a frame or a region and the model propagates the correction through time. Achieving this requires both robust correspondence and efficient inference.

\subsection{Evaluation and Benchmarks}
Beyond perception metrics, controllability should be measured by constraint satisfaction, identity preservation, edit locality, and temporal stability, with standardized test suites that stress compositional prompts and conflicting constraints. For PSG, benchmarks should emphasize rare relations, cross-dataset generalization, and open-world evaluation with language variability~\citep{zellers2018neural, yang2022panoptic, wu2024towards}. Human-in-the-loop protocols are also important: they better capture instruction fidelity, usefulness, and failure modes that are difficult to quantify automatically.

\subsection{Efficiency and Adaptation}
Parameter-efficient tuning, modular control branches, and caching strategies are key to practical deployment. For PSG, lightweight adapters that expand vocabulary without retraining the full model can reduce maintenance cost as label spaces evolve~\citep{wu2024towards, zhou2025openpsg}. For generation, modular controls that can be composed (rather than retrained jointly) may improve reliability and reduce interference among constraints~\citep{zhang2023adding}.

\subsection{Trustworthy Generation}
As control becomes more precise and accessible, safeguards become more important. Provenance tracking and watermarking can support attribution; edit auditing can help users understand what changed; and policy-aware constraints can reduce misuse. From a research standpoint, a key challenge is to integrate safety mechanisms without degrading controllability or encouraging brittle failure modes under benign edits.

\section{Closing Remarks}
The results in this thesis highlight two parallel directions for visual intelligence. Structured understanding is most useful when it is holistic, pixel-grounded, and scalable to open-world vocabularies; controllable generation is most valuable when it preserves the correct reference information faithfully across space and time. Advancing these two directions in parallel is a practical route toward visual systems that are more expressive for reasoning and more dependable for creation.

We hope the methods and perspectives developed here provide a foundation for future work on both structured understanding and controllable generation.
